The concept is simple. You shuffle the deck. You draw.

Every time you shuffle a deck of playing cards, you are drawing from one of [52!](https://nik.art/the-universe-in-your-hand/) possible configurations of the deck to draw the card you see before you.

[Staggering numbers](/blog/2021/3/14/Time.md) to wrap your head around.

These possibilities—this array of alternate timelines you could pull from all point to the same conclusion: they are all illusions. The only one that matters is the one you pulled. That is the only one that is real to you.

# The Only Thing Real Is What's Real To You

Would the card have been different had I shuffled? Likely.

But it doesn't matter. Because that's not what happened. *You* shuffled. And here we are. I can only help you understand the meaning behind the cards. But it's inextricably linked to you. Your past. Your present. And your future. All led to this card presenting itsefl before you now.

Tell me.

What do you see?

# The 3 Card Spread

To begin with, I would simply read the translations from the guide book that came with the Tarot book. Eventually, I learned what each tarot card symbolized and now I have a page of notes for each card where I can jot down my [reflections](/tags/#Reflections).

I didn't bother with upside down cards. That is a bit more complicated, where you interpret the card's energy as "blocked" and attempt to invert it's meaning. I stick to face up cards strictly.

I would start out doing a 3 card spread. Naturally, I assumed it represented the past, present and future. And it can! However, I've since realized that it doesn't necessarily have to be read chronologically from left and right.

You can use the 3 card spread to indicate the source of the issue and the 2 orbiting bodies that surround it.

Eventually, I stopped looking at it in terms of past/future and just accepted that the 3 card spread simply provides more context than the 1 card spread.

I was doing past, present and future readings for girls at that party.

# Measure Randomness

Even if the universe was deterministic and everything happens for a reason, from our perspective, our senses are just so flooded with information from the torrential river of time that we can't help but be caught in the cause and effect loop of the eons of the past.

How then do we orient ourselfs if we're tumbling down a river?

The same way you measure gravity when you are stuck in an avalanche: you spit.

Gravity will pull it down. So whicever way the spit flows is *down*. Towards the Earth. Now, you can orient yourself precisely in 3D space.

A river is slightly different. You spit and it gets washed away in the current. In this case, there are two things you'd want to look out for.

Which direction you are *flowing* in? Close your eyes. Use your sense of inertia. Which direction is the river taking you?

Obviously this would be easier to measure the slower the current is taking you. So perhaps you wait until you get a moment of respite from the river.
